Single sign-on, free on every plan

Single sign-on is often a paid add-on. In DumbSlab it's on the free plan: people sign in with Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, OneLogin, Slack or any OpenID Connect provider, under your organization's own rules.

Free, with no credit card. Every feature is on every plan.

Set up with Google, Microsoft or Slack in one click

There's nothing to register. DumbSlab has its own Google, Microsoft and Slack sign-in apps, and an admin signs in once with the organization's account.

  1. As a workspace admin, open Settings › Single sign-on.
  2. Choose Set up with Google, Set up with Microsoft or Set up with Slack.
  3. Sign in with your organization's account: a Google Workspace account, a Microsoft work or school account, or your Slack workspace's account.
  4. Check the allowed email domains, then try signing in from a private window.

DumbSlab reads your organization from that sign-in: only accounts from your Google Workspace domain, your Microsoft Entra directory or your Slack workspace can sign in.

Okta, OneLogin and any OpenID Connect provider

For Okta, OneLogin or another OpenID Connect provider, register an app with your provider, with your workspace's address plus /sso/callback as its redirect URI, such as https://acme.dumbslab.com/sso/callback. Then fill in its details, client ID and secret under Settings › Single sign-on › Use your own app. DumbSlab checks the provider's discovery document and says whether it all checks out. Your provider's own rules apply, including its multi-factor authentication and which people are assigned the app.

The controls admins need

  • Allowed email domains decide whose accounts can join an existing person on first sign-in.
  • Auto-provisioning, off by default, lets anyone at an allowed domain join as a member the first time they sign in.
  • Require single sign-on lets only admins sign in with a password, as a way in should the provider fail. Everyone else uses the provider, and API tokens and MCP connections keep working.
  • Roles and private topics decide what each person sees once they're in: admin, member or viewer.

No identity provider? Continue with Google or Microsoft

Without any setup, people can sign up and sign in with a Google account, personal or work, or a Microsoft work or school account. An admin still decides who's in: they add people by email, and those people then sign in with Google or Microsoft.

Questions

Is single sign-on really free?

Yes. Single sign-on is on every plan, including Free, with every other feature. The free plan limits only storage and daily database writes.

Does DumbSlab support SAML?

No. DumbSlab uses OpenID Connect, which Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta and OneLogin all support. A provider that only speaks SAML can't be used.

Can we require single sign-on for everyone?

Yes, for everyone but admins. Turn on Require single sign-on in Settings › Single sign-on. Admins keep a password so the workspace stays reachable if the provider has an outage.

Can people sign in with a personal Microsoft account?

Not with DumbSlab's Microsoft app, which serves work and school accounts. Personal Google accounts work with Continue with Google.
